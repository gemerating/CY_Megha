# Cabble

## A · The condition

**The condition.** Food can be designed into an ideal form, nutrition, taste, or experience. Cabble asks what that process changes or takes away from the object and the maker.

**Who lives inside it?** N/A. Cabble is an imagined fruit.

**Evidence gathered so far.**

1. A 2014 [3DPrint.com article about Dovetailed's 3D-printed fruit](https://3dprint.com/4314/3d-printed-fruit-microsoft/) describes combining flavored spheres into customized fruit forms, with the taste and appearance shaped to the user's desire.
2. An [Inhabitat article about 3D-printed fruit](https://inhabitat.com/the-fruit-of-the-future-is-3d-printed-and-packed-with-vitamins-and-minerals/) describes a printed cellulose shell that is filled with nutrient-rich liquids to create a plump, fruit-like form. The excerpt supplied for this project says the dry shell can be flattened for storage and transport. Its sustainability claim is attributed to the designer, not independently verified here.
3. **Still to find:** another source or a documented observation that helps ground what designing or preserving an ideal fruit changes or takes away.

These examples use different processes: the 2014 report describes flavored spheres, while the Inhabitat excerpt describes a cellulose shell and added liquid. They are examples of designed fruit, not evidence that the processes are the same.

**Cabble's question.** If fruit can be designed for a chosen taste, form, nutrition, or longer storage, what is changed or lost in making it ideal?
