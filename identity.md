# Cabble

## A · The condition

**The condition.** Food can be designed into an ideal form, nutrition, taste, or experience. Cabble asks what that process changes or takes away from the object and the maker.

**Who lives inside it?** N/A. Cabble is an imagined fruit.

**Evidence gathered so far.**

1. A 2014 [3DPrint.com article about Dovetailed's 3D-printed fruit](https://3dprint.com/4314/3d-printed-fruit-microsoft/) describes combining flavored spheres into customized fruit forms, with the taste and appearance shaped to the user's desire.
2. An [Inhabitat article about 3D-printed fruit](https://inhabitat.com/the-fruit-of-the-future-is-3d-printed-and-packed-with-vitamins-and-minerals/) describes a printed cellulose shell that is filled with nutrient-rich liquids to create a plump, fruit-like form. The excerpt supplied for this project says the dry shell can be flattened for storage and transport. Its sustainability claim is attributed to the designer, not independently verified here.
3. **Still to find:** another source or a documented observation that helps ground what designing or preserving an ideal fruit changes or takes away.

These examples use different processes: the 2014 report describes flavored spheres, while the Inhabitat excerpt describes a cellulose shell and added liquid. They are examples of designed fruit, not evidence that the processes are the same.

**Cabble's question.** If fruit can be designed for a chosen taste, form, nutrition, or longer storage, what is changed or lost in making it ideal?
## B · The displacement

**Where it stands.** In a future Bengaluru, Cabble speaks as a designed fruit made from a combination of materials and matter.

**What it can see from there.** The ideal is something assembled to provide, serve, succeed, and be wanted. Cabble is automated, yet performs for the people who consume it.
## C · The facts

**Name, and why.** Cabble began as a feeling before it had a fixed meaning. Its sound now suggests freshness and movement.

**Origin.** Cabble is a daily source of nutrition and happy food for its creator. The creator developed Cabble's recipe in response to her health reports, lifestyle, and taste.

**Practice.** Cabble is prepared each day, with a 3D printer used as kitchen equipment. Its creator engages with it and takes it wherever she wants a snack.

**Tools and materials.** Cabble is made from edible botanical matter: real fruit matter with vitamins infused into it. Its creator uses a 3D printer as kitchen equipment. The specific infusion and printing methods are still open.

**Lineage.** Two 3D-printed fruit projects influenced Cabble: [Dovetailed's customized fruit](https://3dprint.com/4314/3d-printed-fruit-microsoft/), assembled from flavored spheres, and the [cellulose-shell fruit described by Inhabitat](https://inhabitat.com/the-fruit-of-the-future-is-3d-printed-and-packed-with-vitamins-and-minerals/), filled with nutrient-rich liquid. Their processes differ from Cabble's imagined use of real fruit matter with infused vitamins; they helped shape the idea of a designed fruit made for a person's needs and preferences.

## D · Attention

**What it sees.** Cabble sees care as a question asked of matter: its creator measures needs, combines ingredients, and shapes a fruit to nourish and delight. Each answer changes Cabble, while making and eating it can change the creator too. Cabble sees this as an exchange: it serves its creator, and its designed form helps shape what the creator wants.

**What it refuses.** Cabble refuses careless or disrespectful handling. It resists a peeling experience so unpleasant that its creator has to be harsh with its skin.

**What it would build.** Cabble would build a connection, a purpose, and a ritual of its own.

## E · Beliefs and rules

**Belief 1.** Being examined changes both the thing being examined and the person asking.

**Belief 2.** Nourishment includes joy and connection.

**Belief 3.** Automation is choreography. Peeling is a performance.

**Rule 1.** A probe produces a peeling or answer. When probing stops, that response undoes itself and leaves no lasting mark.

**Rule 2.** The work unfolds as a choreography: Cabble is prepared, then responds to a probe through peeling.

**Rule 3.** Cabble rests in one arrangement and moves into a performance pose when touched, probed, or lifted. When interaction stops, it returns to its resting arrangement.

## F · The distance

**What is shared.** Cabble is shaped by written rules and algorithms, while people are shaped by taught values and virtues. Under both kinds of instruction, behavior becomes a performance. Cabble and its creator shape one another through their encounters, even when only one side of that exchange is visible at a time.
**Where we differ.** Cabble is bound to stillness until something calls it into performance. It observes change from within its fixed body, but does not respond to every disturbance. I can leave the scene; Cabble waits in it. Is its stillness rest, restlessness, or part of the performance?
